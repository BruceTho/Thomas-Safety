Make every lap count with HR (Horse Race) Game

Two to five teams compete over a shared race period, tracking weekly safety incidents and unexcused absences. One furlong equals one week, and the team with the lowest totals at the finish wins. See how it works below or go directly to the track by taking the Track Side link

The challenge

Small disruptions can slow the whole operation.

These are workplace patterns to understand and improve—not reasons to blame people. Look for causes, offer support, and make the work safer.

A

Attendance gaps

Unplanned absences and late changes can leave shifts short, increase handoffs, and put pressure on coworkers and schedules.

Safety incidents

Injuries, near misses, and hazards can interrupt work, require follow-up, and affect the well-being of the people involved.

Lost momentum

Staffing disruptions and incident response can reduce available work time, delay goals, and make consistent performance harder.

The HR Game concept

Up to five teams. One furlong per week.

Run with as few as two teams. Set the finish from week 4 through week 15, and post the standings after each furlong to keep the teams engaged.

Game rules

How to run the game

Set the rules before the first week, track the same measures for every team, and total the results at the chosen finish.

Form the teams

Create two to five teams of up to 10 employees, then choose a race length from four to 15 weeks.

Record each week

For every team, record that week’s safety incidents and unexcused absences. One furlong on the track represents one week.

Post the weekly standings

Display an update after each furlong so teams can follow the race and stay engaged. Keep postings team-level, not individual.

Determine the winner

At the selected finish week, compare each team’s totals across the full run. The team with the lowest incident and unexcused-absence totals wins.

◆

Make it fair, private, and safety-first.

Use consistent definitions across teams and protect personal information. Weekly postings should be team-level, not individual. Every incident and near miss must still be reported; the competition should never discourage reporting, delay care, or penalize people for raising safety concerns. Review incident totals alongside corrective actions and prevention efforts, and consider attendance data with context and compassion.
